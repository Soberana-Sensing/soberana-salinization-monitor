Accurately predicting the degree and spatial distribution of soil salinization could help prevent and mitigate soil salinization. Satellite remote sensing provides rich spectral information, wide spatial coverage, and long time series data (Tan et al., 2024). It can provide real-time earth surface reflectance information and gradually becomes widely applied to the quantitative prediction of soil physical and chemical properties (Orton et al., 2022). Due to the strong light reflec - tion by the salts (e.g., sodium chloride and sodium sulfate), salinized soil typically exhibits higher spectral reflectance in the visible and near- infrared bands than normal soil. Soda saline-alkali soil exhibits sharply increased spectral reflectance in the 400 to 1300 nm range, and the spectral reflectance increases with increased salinization. This pos - itive correlation enables soil salinization monitoring through remote sensing. Unlike other soil properties, salinization exhibits a unique dy - namic: soluble salts are deposited in deep soil layers during water infiltration but accumulated on the soil surface during water evapora - tion. Remote sensing imaging instantaneously captures the spectral and spatial information at fixed time intervals, and each image provides an individual view of the ground target (Duan et al., 2022). The time- varying states of salts due to environmental factors result in distinct spectral responses. Varying salt and water contents in saline soil cause irregular shifts in spectral absorption peak positions. Thus, mono- temporal imagery data exhibit contingency and randomness. In addi - tion, the complex mixture of solid, liquid, and gas phases in soil exert a coupling effect on the image spectra, rendering it increasingly difficult to accurately predict soil salinity based on mono-temporal imagery data (Wang et al., 2023b). For example, near-infrared spectroscopy is very sensitive to even small amounts of water or hydroxyl groups, resulting in irregular radiative properties. The scattering and transmission effects of surface roughness on soil spectra also significantly alter the spectral data. In particular, large-scale soil salinity mapping often faces diverse land management patterns, various soil types, complex topographic conditions, and diverse environments, leading to distinct spatial het - erogeneity in soil properties. Consequently, the spectral characteristics arising from soil salinity differences cannot be fully captured through mono-temporal imagery, thus limiting the mapping accuracy (Lopez- Lozano et al., 2010). The reliability and integrity of imagery data on the mapping target are key to the accuracy and generalization ability of the prediction model. Time series remote sensing images record the dy - namic characteristics of surface soil salt accumulation and capture suf - ficient surface states and environmental information, thereby reducing the deviation of mapping results (Tan et al., 2024). Thus, this study attempts to fuse multi-temporal Landsat-8 images and evaluate the soil salinity mapping performance based on the fused images.
Pixel-level fusion of multi-temporal images means the fusion of image information from sensors at different imaging times according to specific rules to generate high-precision remote sensing products with enhanced information (Meng et al., 2022b). Common image fusion methods include the discrete Fourier analysis, discrete wavelet trans - form, Laplacian pyramid transform (LPT), and asymmetric Gaussian function fitting. Methods like principal component analysis and discrete Fourier analysis often exhibit deficiencies such as image spectral distortion or peak migration, blurred edge features, and inaccurate reflectance during image sequence processing (Huang et al., 2024). This study performs fusion to capture the dynamics of soil salinization and improve the accuracy of large-scale soil salinity mapping, thus over - coming the limitations of the randomness of mono-temporal data and the uncertainty of the coupling effect of multi-source factors on pixel spectra. Regional differences reportedly mediate the effects of soil properties and environmental factors on the image spectra, e.g., the factors and the influence degrees vary at different scales (Cui et al., 2023). Among these methods, LPT is comparatively more suitable for
fusing multi-temporal images thanks to its ability to decompose the original images into a series of multi-scale high-frequency components and achieve accurate reconstruction. This study improves LPT with an adaptive regional energy weighting (ARW) method, thereby allowing the fusion of each group of sub-images at different scales to reduce the information losses and errors caused by large-scale image fusion. In addition, the regional energy weighting method (Liu et al., 2019) is adopted as a fusion criterion, and the k-nearest neighbors clustering method (Weinberger and Saul, 2009) is employed to improve the par - titioning of fusion regions and prevent the energy mixing of different objects.
Environmental covariates are paramount for assessing soil salinity (Han et al., 2023). According to the SCORPAN framework, controlling factors of soil salinization include vegetation, soil, hydrology, topog - raphy, climate, and human activities. The processes, mechanisms, and degrees of soil salinization exhibit regional heterogeneity and depend on climate, evapotranspiration intensity, and groundwater table (Fan et al., 2022). Arid and semi-arid areas have low precipitation but high evap - oration, facilitating soluble salt accumulation in the surface layer of soil.
Terrain elevations directly affect surface water and groundwater movements and, in turn, salt movement and accumulation. Meanwhile, groundwater level directly affects soil salinization, with shallower groundwater levels favoring the upward groundwater movement to the surface through the capillary action of the soil. With greater water evaporation and loss, more salts accumulate in the surface soil. This soil salt accumulation is especially significant when the groundwater salinity is high. In addition, plant growth and metabolic processes may also affect the distribution of salt in soil. Soil salinity mapping based solely on remote sensing data could neglect the soil properties as well as the mechanism and processes of soil salinization, making it difficult to fully reflect the spatial distribution characteristics of soil salinity (Esmaeilizad et al., 2024). Incorporating terrain, vegetation, and climate parameters as covariates has proved effective in improving soil salinity mapping accuracy (Ge et al., 2022). However, previous studies scarcely considered the dynamics of environmental covariates. Because soil salinization occurrence is spatially clustered while the spatial distribu - tion of environmental covariates is continuous, spatial differences in environment covariates over a short period cannot adequately reveal the causes and distribution characteristics of soil salinization. Given the periodical (annual) changes in environmental covariates like climate and vegetation, selecting environmental parameters in an appropriate period for modeling is essential. Due to data scarcity, limited environ - mental factors were considered previously, especially groundwater covariates. In addition, previous studies focused primarily on improving soil salinity estimation accuracy by training machine learning models on environmental covariates (Ge et al., 2022; Sorenson et al., 2023;
Taghizadeh-Mehrjardi et al., 2021), thus lacking an interpretable anal - ysis of each variable in machine learning prediction to reveal the effects of environmental factors on soil salinity prediction. Accordingly, this study investigates the potential of environmental covariates in soil salinity mapping using monthly climate, soil, and vegetation data in the 2014 to 2023 period, the groundwater and topographic data from 2019, and the land type data from the past decade. Environmental covariates often serve as the core data in traditional digital soil mapping, and remote sensing images are the information carriers of soil spectra (Chen et al., 2022). Incorporating multi-temporal remote sensing images with time-series environmental covariates for soil salinity mapping can minimize the impact of spatiotemporal heterogeneity of soil properties while conform to soil science principles.
This study fuses Landsat-8 images and environmental covariates between 2014 and 2023 to map the soil salinity in the western Jilin Province, China. 2.1.Study areaThe study area is in the western Jilin Province, China (121◦55′–127◦03′ E, 42◦45′–46◦41′ N) and covers 81862.20 km2, including Baicheng, Songyuan, Siping, and Changchun (Fig. 2). This site features flat terrains, with an altitude of 74 to 266 m. The main soil types include Chernozems, Arenosols, and Solonetz. The region has a temperate continental monsoon climate, with an average annual tem-perature of about 3.7℃ and annual mean precipitation of about 443.7 mm. In recent decades, natural and human factors, in combination, have initiated multiple desertification processes, forming one of the three largest soda saline-alkali lands worldwide (Li et al., 2022). Soil salini-zation has led to land abandonment of over 10 % of the cultivated land in this area, with annual expansion rates of 1.1 % to 2.2 %, threatening the local agriculture, animal husbandry, and ecological environment.
2.2.Data2.2.1.Soil sampling and processingA total of 343 soil samples were collected from the study area in April 2023 (Fig. 2a). During sampling, the land had no snow or weeds, and the sowing had just begun, which was regarded as the bare soil period. All samples were collected from wasteland and cultivated land. Nine sub-samples were acquired using a ring knife (3 cm in depth and 200 ml in volume) in each quadrant (Fig. 2b). These samples were transferred to the laboratory for drying. Then, each group of subsamples was mixed, ground, and sieved to a size of ≤0.15 mm for soil electrical conductivity (EC) measurement. A 1:5 soil/water suspension was prepared at room temperature (22.6℃). SevenExcellence S400-Micro multi-parameter measuring instrument (METTLER TOLEDO, USA) was used to measure the suspension EC. Images with the least cloud cover were selected to maximize the available part of each image, and ten (>20 % cloud coverage) were excluded accordingly. The images selected were mainly those captured in April so that the impact of vegetation cover can be minimized (Table A1). The spatial resolution of the Landsat-8 OLI multispectral image is 30 m, with seven spectral channels at 440 nm to 2300 nm. Among them, spectral data from six channels except band 1 (coastal band: 440 nm) were used. The images were atmospherically corrected using Radiometric Calibration and FLAASH in Environment for Visualizing Images to obtain the original reflectance data. The ARW-LPT method fuses Landsat-8 images in the bare soil periods from 2014 to 2023 (Fig. 3). As a multi-scale image fusion method, LPT includes image decomposition (Eq. (1)and reconstruction (Eq. (2). It decomposes the original images into different layers by extracting the high-frequency components of them using the Laplace operator (Yin and Xiao, 2022). The Landsat-8 image band was divided into five levels by LPT, from low frequency to high frequency. Regional energy weighting (Eq. (3)was used to fuse bands of the same level. Due to irregular edge features in the low-frequency and high-frequency components, fusing by dividing the local area into grids for region energy weighting calculation tends to increase noise or cause information loss (Lv et al., 2023). An adaptive clustering method based on the k-nearest neighbor algorithm was used to ensure consistent types of ground objects in the partitioned area and circumvent the problems of ghosting and seams. Adaptive K- means clustering can dynamically adjust the K value by calculating the silhouette coefficient of each cluster based on the clustering results during iteration. By observing the trend of contour coefficient and inertia with the change of K value, the K value at the inflection point is selected as the optimal number of clusters. Based on the fused band at each level, the band was reconstructed to obtain the final fused band. The above process was repeated for the six bands of the Landsat-8 im-ages, and the image fusion was complete. 2.4.Variable selection algorithmSince some bands and environmental variables are unrelated to soil salinization, feature extraction is necessary for soil salinity mapping. This study combined eXtreme gradient boosting (XG-Boost) and SHapley Additive exPlanation (SHAP) to rank the feature importance of variables and introduced the shadow features of the Boruta algorithm (Amiri et al., 2019) to determine the importance threshold.XG-Boost is a boosting-based ensemble learning model that in-tegrates CART trees into a strong learner (Chen et al., 2016). In each CART tree, XG-Boost calculates the importance and weight of the fea-tures and selects the best features and nodes to split. Grid search is used to determine the optimal parameters for XG-Boost. The n_estimators, max_depth, and learning_rate were set to 220, 12, and 0.3, respectively. XG-Boost offers significantly improved training speed and prediction accuracy than traditional gradient boosting trees due to its use of splitting calculation, node splitting sorting, and second-order Taylor expansion. XG-Boost is favored due to its ability to rank feature importance according to their contributions to the model. Despite this advantage, it possesses complex operation logic and black-box properties.The SHAP algorithm is proposed by Lundberg et al. to explain ma-chine learning models based on additional feature attributes. Derived from the game theory, SHAP explains the contribution of each factor to the output from a machine learning model. This method assigns a SHAP value to each feature by combining feature subsets and calculating the average marginal contribution of different feature subsets to the pre-diction results. Using SHAP to explain the XG-Boost model has the following two unique advantages: 1) The SHAP interaction value en-sures the consistency of interpreting the feature interaction effect, and 2) SHAP can identify whether the contribution of each input feature is positive or negative, unlike the existing importance feature analysis in machine learning models.
2.5.Convolutional neural network (CNN)CNN is a forward neural network structure comprising the convo-lution, pooling, and fully connected layers. The convolution layer con-volves each local feature of the input image through the convolution kernel and extracts the feature information. With the pooling layer, the model overfitting is alleviated by decreasing the feature dimensionality (Shin et al., 2016). Through repeated adjustments, this study con-structed a CNN with three convolution layers, three pooling layers, and two fully connected layers, with each convolutional layer followed by a pooling layer. Model parameters such as epoch and learning rate were determined via Bayesian optimization (Table 2). According to the data set size and data characteristics, through repeated experiments, the search ranges of kernel size, stride, epoch, learning rate, and batch size are 2–9, 2–5, 5–50, 0.01–1, 16/32/64/128, respectively. The padding was set to ’valid’. Dropout and early stop strategies were introduced into the training process to avoid overfitting. Regarding mapping pre-cision, the soil EC values predicted by fused images are noticeably better than those predicted using mono-temporal images, especially when environmental covariates are incorporated. Spatially, mapping results based on mono-temporal images cannot reflect the spatial distribution heterogeneity of soil EC. The area with serious soil salinization is virtually concentrated in the northwest. However, the model over-estimates the soil EC value in the central part of the study area due to limited information in the mono-temporal images and the strong coupling effect of multiple soil attributes on the spectrum. Although introducing environmental covariates reduces this error, the limited spatial resolution of climate and groundwater covariates results in a gap phenomenon when mapping soil EC. The mapping effect based on fused images is relatively effective, reflecting areas of severe soil salinization. Despite such an improvement, the discrete distribution remains pro-nounced. The prediction model that considers both time-series images and environmental covariates achieves the optimal mapping effect on soil EC. Specifically, the predicted distribution of soil EC is relatively continuous and exhibits clustering features. These patterns are consis-tent with the distribution characteristics of soil salinization areas. Overall, combining multi-temporal images and environmental cova-riates for soil EC prediction proves advantageous in prediction accuracy and mapping capability.4.Discussion4.1.The advantages of fused multi-temporal imagesThe pixel-level fusion method for multi-temporal remote sensing images can improve the detection ability for soil salinity (Han et al., 2022). An ARW-LPT method was proposed to fuse the Landsat-8 images from 2014 to 2023 during the “bare soil period”. Using the proposed method, the Laplacian operator decomposes the original image into a pyramid of one low-frequency and four multi-scale high-frequency components. Subsequently, the fusion region is divided based on the k- nearest neighbor clustering method, and the regional energy weighting serves as the rule to fuse the components of the same scale. Compared with other fusion approaches, ARW-LPT allows image fusion at various frequencies, thus fully considering the differences in the spectral response of multiple soil properties at different scales. Moreover, it does not mix energy from ground objects and considers adjacent pixels, thereby obtaining more reliable spectral characteristics of soil pixels. As expected by Han et al. (2022)and Azabdaftari and Sunar (2016), fusing multi-temporal remote sensing images facilitates the improvement of soil parameter prediction accuracy. Compared with other soil proper-ties, soil salinization has unique dynamic characteristics. Specifically, soluble salts deposit in the soil or crystallize on the soil surface when soil moisture leaches in or evaporates (Fig. 11). In large regional-scale soil salinity mapping, soil salt dynamics are random due to the envi-ronment. Soil samples with internal salt deposition have higher salt content and weaker spectral characteristics. As a result, the estimation model based on mono-temporal images overestimates soil salinity in non-saline areas. Multi-temporal images record the spatiotemporal dy-namic characteristics of soil salinity. Image fusion integrates these spectral characteristics at the pixel scale, considering salt crystallization and deposition. This reduces the degree of soil moisture dispersion and reflects more noticeable spectral characteristics of saline soil. Typically, more fused images produce better soil parameter pre-dictions. Inconsistently, it is found that the soil EC prediction accuracy does not increase monotonically with the number of fused images. When the images of 2021 were fused, the soil EC prediction accuracy decreased. This decline may also be related to soil moisture distribution. In the spring of 2021, a flood event occurred in the Nenjiang River, further enhancing the soil moisture spatial heterogeneity of the west and east of the study area (Wu et al., 2022). For this reason, image selection before fusion is necessary to ensure the desired fusion effect. Our method emphasizes the comprehensive consideration of multi-temporal images to reduce the bias of mono-temporal remote sensing data, thereby improving soil property prediction. 4.2.The influences of environmental covariatesThe SCORPAN framework emphasizes that environmental variables greatly improve soil salinity prediction accuracy. This study found that topography, soil, vegetation, climate, land type, and groundwater environmental covariates determine the soil salinization degree. SHAP was adopted to reveal the effects of various environmental covariates on soil salinity. Specifically, TWI, DI, NDSI, SI, PRE, and TEM make sig-nificant positive contributions to soil EC; by comparison, DEM, GWD, SAVI, ET, LUDCI, and TR offer significant negative contributions (Fig. 13). To delve into how different environmental covariates influ-ence soil salinity, their contribution rates to soil EC prediction accuracy were determined using the stepwise regression method (Fig. 14). The contributions of environmental covariates to soil EC prediction accuracy before and after image fusion differ, and the cumulative contribution rates reach 10.53 % and 12.47 %, respectively. Each pixel of the fused image reflects the bare soil information from 2014 to 2023. Given that soil properties are jointly controlled by multi-source environmental factors, fused images also record such environmental information. Regardless of whether image fusion is applied, introducing topography variables improves soil EC prediction accuracy the most, followed by inputting groundwater, soil, climate, vegetation, and land type vari-ables. Due to the abundant surface water resources in areas with serious soil salinization (in the northwest of the investigation site), such as the Nenjiang River and Chagan Lake, topographic factors become the decisive elements in controlling and regulating hydrological processes that govern salt transport. Water migration brought the salts to low- lying areas, causing them to gradually accumulate on the soil surface. In addition, the terrain also affects salt distribution. Salts with high solubility can be carried farther by runoff, while those with low solu-bility can be carried to closer areas. The contribution rates of topog-raphy variables to soil EC prediction accuracy based on mono-temporal and fusion images are 4.67 % and 4.19 %, respectively, consistent with the conclusion of Ge et al. (2022)that DEM and TWI were important covariates for soil salinity monitoring around lakes. Among the soil environment covariates, NDSI exhibits the highest contribution. The increased soil water stress drives the capillary action of plant roots and soil pores, resulting in salt accumulation in deep soil layers or ground-water in the topsoil with upward water migration (Litalien and Zeeb, 2020). In addition, the DI determines the degree of climatic aridity and is closely related to soil salinity. During droughts, water moves upward from the deep soil through the capillary action to supplement the water evaporation loss of the surface soil, thus bringing up the soluble salts and accumulating them on the soil surface. The decreased PRE and increased ET and TEM may aggravate soil salinization. Image fusion reduces the likelihood of soil salinity prediction results falling into local optimum by capturing the spectral dynamics of soil salinity during crystallization and sedimentation pro-cesses. In addition, considering the coupling effect of multiple envi-ronmental factors on soil properties can improve the generalization and interpretability of the prediction model. Compared with traditional remote sensing estimation methods for soil salinity using spatiotemporal fusion algorithms (Han et al., 2022) or combined with environmental covariates (Ge et al., 2022), the “multi-temporal image fusion +multi- source environmental covariates” strategy can minimize the impact of spatiotemporal heterogeneity introduced by soil properties and conform with the principles of soil science. This approach provides a paradigm for future research on soil physicochemical property prediction.Despite the promising prediction potential, this method has two limitations. (1) The optimal fusion effect relies on multi-temporal im-ages fully covering the area of interest. It is challenging to meet this requirement due to cloud cover, and fusing images from different sen-sors can help address this challenge (Wang et al., 2023a). However, the error induced by this approach needs further assessment. Similarly, the spatiotemporal effect of this method on soil salinity monitoring requires evaluation. (2) The spatial resolution of environmental covariates is limited, and the environmental data obtained by interpolation contain errors. The presence of errors may be the primary cause of the low contribution rates of some environmental covariates to soil salinity prediction accuracy. In addition, the inconsistent time scales of the included environmental covariates (e.g., groundwater and topography) may also cause errors.5.ConclusionsThis study incorporated multi-temporal remote sensing images with environmental covariates to explore the soil salinity mapping perfor-mance. Therefore, effective monitoring and assessment of soil salinization are crucial for implementing appropriate mitigation strategies. Optical remote sensing satellites can achieve rapid regional topsoil salinization mapping. However, previous studies rarely considered the soluble salt deposition or crystallization dynamics in soils, especially at large regional scales, where the existence states of soluble salts are uncertain due to environmental differences. As a result, soil salinity mapping based on mono-temporal images exhibits low accuracy and strong contingency. In light of this, multi-temporal image fusion is considered in this study, and an adaptive regional energy weighting Laplacian pyramid transform (ARW-LPT) method is proposed to fuse Landsat-8 images covering the bare soil periods over nearly a decade to fully exploit the spectral characteristics of soil salinity. Considering the different soil salinization processes, mechanisms, and degrees in different regions, various environmental covariates related to soil salinization, such as topography, groundwater, soil, vegetation, and climate, are incorporated to enhance the generalizability and interpretability of the prediction model. The study area is in the western Jilin Province, China, covering 81862.20 km2, and 343 soil samples are collected. Feature importance of the fusion bands and environmental covariates is assessed using eXtreme gradient boosting and SHapley Additive exPla-nation analysis. A soil electrical conductivity prediction model is established using a convolutional neural network. The results show that 1) the accuracy of soil salinity prediction based on multi-temporal fusion images is higher than that based on mono-temporal images, with an R2 increase of 0.19, an RMSEP decrease of 3.10 ds⋅m 1, and an RPIQ increase of 0.80. 2) Incorporating environmental covariates leads to a better performance in revealing the spatial variability of soil salinity, with the contributions of different environmental covariates to prediction accuracy ranked as topography >groundwater >soil >climate >vegetation >land type. 3) Combining multi-temporal images and environmental covariates leads to the highest soil salinity prediction accuracy and mapping ability, with an R2 of 0.79, an RMSEP of 5.69 ds⋅m 1, and an RPIQ of 2.72. This study highlights the importance of considering the changes in relevant environmental factors and spectral variations during soil salt accumulation, thereby shedding more light on the soil salinization mechanisms and informing soil salinity prediction.1.IntroductionSoil salinization is a leading cause of land desertification and degradation (Wang et al., 2022). It arises from the progressive salt accumulation in the surface soil and is prevalent in areas with arid cli-mates, intense soil evaporation, and high groundwater tables (Xiao et al., 2023). Globally, saline soil covers nearly one billion hectares, approximately 7 % of the total land area, with widespread distributions across Asia, America, and Africa (Ghorbani et al., 2019). Meanwhile, over 77 million hectares of land are threatened by soil salinization, diminishing the yield of about a third of irrigated farmland.
